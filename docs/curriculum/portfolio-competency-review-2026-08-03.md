# Portfolio Competency Review (Level 4 Analysis)

Date: 2026-08-03
Scope: Volume 1 and Volume 2 portfolio competency architecture review
Review type: Analytical review (not direct redesign)

## 1) Executive Summary

The competency architecture is directionally aligned with the intended developmental pathway (Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning -> Professional Judgment), but the current measurable portfolio behaves more like a partially connected partition than a consistently visible continuum.

Key conclusion:

- At portfolio level, competency balance is acceptable.
- At sequence level, transition visibility is uneven.
- Statistical Reasoning is present and often strong, but not consistently functioning as the explicit bridge between execution and judgment across all chapters.
- Full programme competency progression cannot be empirically validated yet because active measured LO inventory currently covers Chapters 1-6 only.

This is a progression-visibility problem, not a complete architecture failure.

## 2) Portfolio-Level Competency Findings

Measured active chapter-level LO set:

- Total active LOs: 103
- Chapters represented in active measured set: 1-6
- Competency totals:
  - Technical Skills: 40
  - Statistical Reasoning: 39
  - Professional Judgment: 24

Interpretation:

- Technical Skills and Statistical Reasoning are near parity.
- Professional Judgment is meaningfully present but smaller, which is educationally plausible for Volume 1.
- The system avoids pure technical dominance at programme aggregate level.
- The main weakness is transition quality and chapter-to-chapter pacing, not raw count imbalance.

## 3) Technical Skills Analysis

### Volume and distribution

- Technical Skills count: 40/103
- Strong presence in every chapter.
- Highest chapter concentrations in Regression Analysis and Goodness of Fit by absolute count.

### Educational assessment

Strengths:

- Provides robust execution foundation.
- Supports procedural readiness for statistical work.

Risks:

- In some advanced contexts, Technical Skills framing still captures outcomes that are primarily interpretive.
- This can crowd out visible bridge wording and make progression appear flatter than intended.

Potential bottleneck pattern:

- Where execution LOs are not explicitly linked to evidential meaning, students may complete procedures without demonstrating transition to reasoning.

## 4) Statistical Reasoning Analysis

### Volume and distribution

- Statistical Reasoning count: 39/103
- Strongest chapter share in Hypothesis Testing (Chapter 4), where SR dominates local profile.

### Educational role

Strengths:

- SR is not underrepresented overall.
- Several chapters use SR to interpret assumptions, uncertainty, and inferential meaning.

Weaknesses:

- SR is not always expressed as an explicit bridge from TS outputs.
- In some chapters, SR is present as a category but less visible as a developmental transition mechanism.

Bridge function judgment:

- SR functions as a partial bridge, not yet a uniformly explicit bridge architecture.

## 5) Professional Judgment Analysis

### Volume and timing

- Professional Judgment count: 24/103
- Present in all represented chapters, but with major local variation.
- Chapter 4 has a marked dip (1/16), followed by higher presence in Chapters 5 and 6.

### Educational assessment

Strengths:

- PJ is included early enough to establish evidential judgment habits.
- Later chapters (especially Goodness of Fit) show stronger PJ expression.

Risks:

- Sequence-level pacing can feel abrupt because local chapter PJ intensity is uneven.
- If SR-to-PJ bridges are not explicit, PJ can appear as isolated endpoints rather than cumulative development.

Volume 1 vs Volume 2 concern:

- Philosophy and governance position PJ growth into Volume 2, but active measured LO dataset does not yet provide full Volume 2 LO evidence in the same registry slice.

## 6) Bridge-Transition Analysis

### Observed transition signals

Heuristic transition indicators found in active set:

- TS -> SR transition-like objectives: 10
- SR -> PJ transition-like objectives: 9

Interpretation:

- Bridge transitions exist but are limited relative to portfolio size.
- Missing bridge language is a major reason the architecture can look category-clean but developmentally under-signaled.

### Transition quality summary

- Strong transitions: selected objectives in Chapters 5 and 6.
- Weak transitions: foundational and mixed chapters where execution and interpretation are adjacent but not explicitly linked.
- Missing transitions: objectives that state procedure without evidential interpretation endpoint.
- Abrupt transitions: points where PJ appears with limited explicit SR bridge in immediate predecessors.

## 7) Volume 1 Findings

Volume 1 competency architecture is broadly functional and progression-aware.

Strengths:

- All three competencies are represented.
- Aggregate TS/SR/PJ pattern is plausible for a statistically grounded first volume.

Weaknesses:

- Chapter-to-chapter competency pacing is uneven.
- Transition visibility from TS to SR and SR to PJ is inconsistent.
- Some LO classifications likely mask true interpretive or evaluative demand.

Overall Volume 1 judgment:

- Moderate to strong architecture.
- Moderate implementation visibility.

## 8) Volume 2 Findings

Current evidence state:

- Volume 2 educational philosophy is documented.
- Active measured LO portfolio used in this review currently represents Chapters 1-6 only.

Implication:

- Volume 2 competency progression cannot be fully validated empirically from the active registry slice analyzed here.

Governance tension to resolve:

- Volume 2 philosophy text references a fourth competency concept.
- LO authoring governance keeps a fixed three-competency model and explicitly places Audit Judgment under Professional Judgment.

This should be harmonized before expanded Volume 2 competency analytics.

## 9) Chapter-by-Chapter Competency Review

### Probability Distributions

- Balanced TS/SR with limited but present PJ.
- Appropriate for foundational chapter purpose.

### Estimation

- Similar balanced profile.
- Reasonable early progression pattern.

### Estimation with Auxiliary Variables and Stratification

- TS-heavy relative to its bridge potential.
- Opportunity to increase explicit SR bridging language.

### Hypothesis Testing

- SR-dominant and pedagogically coherent for inferential interpretation.
- Very low PJ share creates a sequence-level dip that should be explicitly justified in progression narrative.

### Regression Analysis

- Clear candidate for stronger explicit SR and SR->PJ bridges.

### Goodness of Fit

- Better integration of TS, SR, PJ in evidence-evaluation context.

## 11) Competency Reclassification Recommendations

Recommendation type: review proposals, not automatic remapping.

1. Run a targeted competency adjudication set for the eight candidates above.
2. Apply outcome-demand evidence from assessments, not verb-only heuristics.
3. Where objectives bundle execution and interpretation, prefer either:
- split objective design in future revision cycles, or
- explicit primary/secondary interpretation in review notes.
4. Prioritize reclassifications that improve transition visibility rather than balancing counts mechanically.

## 12) Competency Progression Analysis

Core question: continuum or partition?

Judgment:

- Current architecture is between the two states.
- It is not a rigid taxonomy partition, because cross-competency development is present.
- It is not yet a consistently visible continuum, because many transitions are implicit and chapter pacing is uneven.

Visibility:

- Moderate.

Coherence:

- Moderate to strong at aggregate level.

Sequencing and pacing:

- Moderate; one notable local discontinuity in Chapter 4 PJ share.

Reinforcement:

- Stronger in later represented chapters, especially Goodness of Fit.

## 13) Governance Implications

1. Progression-governance intent is in force and appropriate.
2. Current competency usage mostly supports philosophy but under-signals transitions.
3. Bridge Objective Standard should be used as the primary mechanism to connect competencies educationally.
4. Local compliance should continue to be supplemented by sequence-level competency review.
5. Volume 2 competency language should be harmonized with fixed-model governance before further portfolio expansion.

## 14) Redesign Priorities (Future Review Candidates)

### High Priority

1. Full-programme competency observability
- Integrate Volume 2 active LO evidence into the same authoritative measured portfolio.

2. Transition visibility in advanced technical chapters
- Increase explicit TS->SR and SR->PJ bridge articulation in objective wording and assessment evidence.

3. Targeted competency adjudication pass
- Resolve high-impact likely misclassifications that distort progression interpretation.

### Medium Priority

1. Hypothesis Testing chapter progression narrative
- Explicitly justify low PJ share as developmental staging or rebalance gradually.

2. Regression chapter competency signaling
- Reduce technical over-signaling where outcomes are primarily evidential interpretation.

3. Symmetry risk monitoring
- Track repeated competency mixes for template-driven drift.

### Low Priority

1. Minor distribution tuning once transition quality is improved.
2. Cosmetic count balancing where chapter purpose is already clear.

## 15) Strategic Recommendations

1. Treat competency categories as developmental states, not buckets.
2. Use bridge-objective quality as the main lever for progression strength.
3. Prioritize transition clarity over symmetrical competency percentages.
4. Require chapter-level statements that explain each chapter's role in TS->SR->PJ progression.
5. Run dual-lens governance checks:
- local LO compliance,
- programme progression integrity.

Final strategic judgment:

The competency architecture can support the intended educational progression, but only if transition visibility is made explicit and sequence-level pacing is actively governed. At present, the programme shows promising progression structure with incomplete developmental signaling.
