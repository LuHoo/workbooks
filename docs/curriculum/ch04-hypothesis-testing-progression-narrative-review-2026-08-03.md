# Chapter 4 Hypothesis Testing Progression Narrative Review (Issue 282)

Date: 2026-08-03  
Scope: Curriculum-progression review only (no chapter redesign)

## Executive Determination

Core review question: Is the low Professional Judgment (PJ) representation in Chapter 4 a curriculum problem or an intentional staging mechanism?

Decision: **B. Intentional developmental staging mechanism**, with one governance gap: the rationale is not yet explicit enough in curriculum documentation.

This is therefore a **narrative visibility issue**, not a competency-balance defect.

## 1) Assessment of Chapter 4 Intended Curriculum Role

Chapter 4 is the inferential interpretation pivot in Volume 1. Its purpose is to move students from executing tests toward reasoning about uncertainty, evidential support, and limitation boundaries.

Primary chapter role:

- translate test mechanics into inferential meaning;
- prevent over-claiming from significance results;
- establish disciplined language for what evidence supports and does not support.

This role is inherently Statistical Reasoning-led and is not intended to be a high-PJ chapter.

## 2) Assessment of Current Competency Architecture

Current Chapter 4 profile (active chapter-level set):

- Technical Skills: 6
- Statistical Reasoning: 9
- Professional Judgment: 1
- Total: 16

Architecture interpretation:

- TS remains present as execution foundation (sample size, test execution, MUS procedures).
- SR is dominant and coherent with chapter purpose (p-value interpretation, validity conditions, design-outcome sensitivity, non-significance interpretation).
- PJ appears as a bounded endpoint (LO-C4-16), consistent with Volume 1 boundary rules.

Overall architecture quality: strong and purpose-aligned.

## 3) Evaluation of the Professional Judgment Dip

Observed dip:

- Chapter 4 has lower PJ share than adjacent progression contexts.

Educational interpretation:

- The dip is defensible because Chapter 4 is the formal inferential-discipline chapter, where the core developmental task is SR maturation.
- Forcing PJ expansion here risks collapsing the intended TS -> SR -> PJ sequence into premature audit-response framing.

Governance interpretation (CURR-022 compatible):

- Chapter purpose overrides symmetry.
- Local dips are acceptable if they serve conceptual progression.
- The current dip is acceptable but should be explicitly documented as intentional.

## 4) Detailed Review of LO-C4-15

Current objective:

- LO-C4-15
- Current competency: Statistical Reasoning
- Current text: evaluate whether observed test results support a conclusion of statistical consistency with the stated misstatement threshold, including explicit limitations.

Educational demand analysis:

- Requires inferential evaluation under uncertainty and threshold framing.
- Requires limitation-aware conclusion discipline.
- Does not require selecting audit responses or broader engagement-level action.

Assessment implications:

- Valid evidence of success should include: conclusion statement, limitation statement, and uncertainty rationale.
- If graded only as a binary pass/fail test decision, the objective will be under-assessed relative to its SR demand.

Progression implications:

- LO-C4-15 functions as a high-value SR capstone and prepares transfer into Chapter 5 expectation-evidence evaluation.
- It is also a controlled SR -> PJ pre-bridge, but not a full PJ objective.

Relationship to SR:

- Primary and appropriate.

Relationship to PJ:

- Adjacent but bounded; PJ emerges in LO-C4-16 through communication and evidential boundary articulation.

Adjudication decision:

- Keep current competency classification (Statistical Reasoning).
- Do not reclassify for count reasons.
- Objective splitting is not required now.
- Wording refinement may improve bridge visibility (optional).

Optional wording refinement (non-structural):

- "evaluate whether observed test results support a statistically defensible conclusion of consistency with the stated misstatement threshold, and explicitly state what remains unsupported."

## 5) Progression Analysis

Intended transition emphasis in Chapter 4:

- primarily **Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning**;
- secondarily introduces a bounded **Statistical Reasoning -> Professional Judgment** endpoint.

Currently visible transition:

- TS -> SR is strongly visible.
- SR -> PJ is intentionally light but present via LO-C4-16.

Mismatch judgment:

- no substantive mismatch in educational design;
- mild documentation mismatch (intent not explicitly narrated).

## 6) Transition-Visibility Analysis

Across sequence:

- Chapter 3: method selection and estimation reasoning.
- Chapter 4: inferential interpretation discipline.
- Chapter 5: expectation construction and evidence evaluation.
- Chapter 6: anomaly interpretation and evidential limitations.

Visibility judgment:

- Chapter 4 currently operates as a meaningful bridge between Chapters 3 and 5.
- Students can infer the pathway, but progression intent should be made explicit in chapter-role documentation to reduce interpretation variance across instructors.

## 7) Bridge-Objective Analysis

TS -> SR bridges in Chapter 4:

- Strong and sufficient (especially LO-C4-08/09/10 feeding LO-C4-11/12/13/14/15).

SR -> PJ bridges in Chapter 4:

- Present but intentionally minimal (primarily LO-C4-15 -> LO-C4-16).

Sufficiency judgment:

- Current bridge density is appropriate for chapter purpose.
- Need is not more PJ bridges, but clearer articulation of why SR dominance is deliberate.

## 8) Relationship to Chapters 3, 5, and 6

Chapter 3 -> Chapter 4:

- Chapter 3 develops estimation and method-choice reasoning.
- Chapter 4 converts this into inferential discipline under explicit error-risk framing.

Chapter 4 -> Chapter 5:

- Chapter 4 teaches what inferential claims are justified.
- Chapter 5 applies that discipline to model-based expectations and recorded-vs-expected evaluation.

Chapter 4 -> Chapter 6:

- Chapter 4 establishes non-overclaiming norms (evidence is not proof).
- Chapter 6 extends those norms to anomaly interpretation and explanation limits.

## 9) Relationship to Volume 2 Chapter 1

Volume 2 Chapter 1 is where response selection and consequential audit actions should become central.

Chapter 4 boundary contribution:

- provides disciplined evidence interpretation language;
- avoids premature migration into action-selection outcomes;
- protects progression integrity by staging PJ intensification later.

## 10) Governance Alignment Assessment

Alignment with educational philosophy:

- strong: Perform -> Interpret -> Evaluate is visible in chapter LO sequence.

Alignment with competency progression:

- strong: TS to SR dominates; PJ appears as bounded endpoint.

Alignment with CURR-022 principles:

- strong in design intent;
- partial in documentation explicitness (narrative needs to be recorded).

Alignment with Bridge Objective Standard:

- TS -> SR bridge quality is good.
- SR -> PJ bridge is intentionally narrow and appropriately bounded.

## 11) Risks and Trade-offs

If PJ is increased mechanically in Chapter 4:

- risk of premature audit-response framing;
- risk of weakened SR consolidation;
- risk of progression collapse (TS directly to PJ).

If current profile is maintained without explicit narrative:

- risk that reviewers misread the PJ dip as a defect;
- risk of inconsistent teaching emphasis across cohorts.

## 12) Recommended Actions

1. Maintain Chapter 4 competency architecture as-is (no count-driven rebalance).
2. Keep LO-C4-15 as Statistical Reasoning.
3. Keep LO-C4-16 as bounded Professional Judgment endpoint.
4. Add explicit progression-role narrative to curriculum documentation (see Section 13).
5. Optionally apply minor wording refinement to LO-C4-15 to make limitation-bound inference more explicit.
6. In assessment guidance, require explicit "supported vs unsupported" conclusion statements for LO-C4-15/16 evidence tasks.

## 13) Draft Chapter Progression Narrative (Documentation-Ready)

Proposed narrative text:

"Chapter Hypothesis Testing is the programme's inferential-discipline chapter. Its role is not to maximize Professional Judgment outcomes, but to consolidate Statistical Reasoning by teaching students how to interpret uncertainty, error risk, and test limitations without over-claiming. In progression terms, the chapter primarily advances the Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning transition, while introducing a bounded Statistical Reasoning -> Professional Judgment endpoint through explicit communication of what evidence supports and does not support. This staging is intentional and aligns with CURR-022: chapter purpose takes precedence over competency symmetry, and local Professional Judgment dips are acceptable when they strengthen conceptual progression toward later expectation-evaluation (Chapter Regression Analysis), anomaly-interpretation (Chapter Goodness of Fit), and audit-response decision making in Volume 2."
