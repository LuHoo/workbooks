# CURR-022: Curriculum Progression Governance

Status: Draft Decision Record  
Decision ID: CURR-022  
Decision Type: Curriculum Governance  
Scope: Programme-wide (Volume 1 and Volume 2)  
Related decisions: CURR-004, CURR-005, CURR-008, CURR-014, CURR-021  
Owner: Curriculum Governance  
Proposed date: 2026-08-03

## 1. Proposed Decision Statement

The programme adopts Curriculum Progression Governance as a binding design and review principle.

Curriculum quality will be evaluated not only by chapter-level compliance, but by the visibility and integrity of developmental progression across chapter sequences.

Progression is defined primarily as conceptual and cognitive development in student capability, aligned to:

Perform -> Interpret -> Evaluate

Bloom and competency classifications are required indicators, but they are diagnostic instruments rather than the governing definition of progression.

Chapter design remains purpose-led. Symmetry between chapters is not a default quality target and must be justified when present.

Advanced technical chapters must include explicit bridge objectives that connect technical execution to evidential interpretation.

Reviewers are authorized to require remediation when curriculum-level progression is weak, even where local chapter standards are met.

## 2. Problem Statement

Curriculum review found the programme broadly sound in aggregate, but progression is less visible than intended because chapter architectures are overly regularized.

Observed problems include:

- chapter structures that are too symmetrical;
- Bloom profiles that are too similar across chapters;
- competency profiles that are too similar across chapters;
- advanced chapters retaining too much foundational framing;
- insufficient bridge objectives from execution to interpretation;
- chapter-level compliance masking curriculum-level progression weakness.

## 3. Rationale

The programme aim is developmental: students must progress from performing statistical procedures to using statistical evidence for defensible audit conclusions.

If governance focuses on local compliance and chapter symmetry alone, the curriculum can appear balanced while failing to produce visible developmental progression.

CURR-022 therefore establishes progression governance that preserves educational judgment, avoids rigid metrics, and protects chapter-specific purpose.

## 4. Governing Principles

1. Progression is primarily conceptual and developmental.
2. Bloom and competency distributions are indicators, not progression itself.
3. Chapter educational purpose governs chapter LO architecture.
4. Curriculum balance does not override chapter purpose.
5. Local compliance is necessary but not sufficient for curriculum quality.
6. Progression must be reviewed across chapter sequences, not only within chapters.
7. Symmetry must be justified, not assumed.
8. Bridge objectives are mandatory in advanced technical chapters.
9. Reviewer intervention is authorized where progression is weak.
10. Guidance is principle-based, not numerically rigid.

## 5. Definitions

Curriculum progression:
Planned, observable student development in cognitive and evidential capability across the programme.

Chapter purpose:
The chapter-specific educational role that determines what students should be able to do after completing that chapter.

Curriculum-level progression quality:
The degree to which chapter sequence produces visible developmental movement from Perform to Interpret to Evaluate.

Bridge objective:
A learning objective that explicitly links method execution to evidential interpretation or interpretation to reliability evaluation.

Excessive regularization:
A condition where chapter LO architecture is overly uniform in structure, Bloom profile, or competency profile, reducing progression visibility.

Exception chapter:
A chapter whose profile legitimately departs from immediate stepwise complexity increase because of technique-family introduction or conceptual reset requirements.

## 6. Curriculum Progression Framework

### 6.1 Developmental spine

The governing progression spine is:

Perform -> Interpret -> Evaluate

### 6.2 Competency progression

Primary programme progression:

Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning -> Professional Judgment

Volume guidance:

- Volume 1: establishes technical reliability and evidential interpretation foundations.
- Volume 2: operationalizes professional judgment in decision-oriented audit contexts.

### 6.3 Sequence-level expectation

Progression must be demonstrated across chapter pathways, not chapter snapshots.

Examples of pathways:

- foundational estimation -> design-enhanced estimation -> inferential interpretation;
- model construction -> model interpretation -> evidential adequacy evaluation.

## 7. Relationship with Bloom Taxonomy

Bloom remains required and governance-relevant under CURR-021.

Under CURR-022:

- Bloom distributions are interpreted as diagnostic signals.
- Similar Bloom distributions across adjacent chapters are not automatically defects.
- Similarity becomes a governance concern when it obscures intended developmental movement.
- Review judgments must reference chapter purpose and sequence context.

Policy consequence:
Bloom-based audit findings must include narrative progression analysis, not only percentage summaries.

## 8. Relationship with Competency Classifications

Competency categories remain fixed under CURR-021:

- technical_skills
- statistical_reasoning
- professional_judgment

Under CURR-022:

- competency distributions are interpreted as evidence markers;
- chapter-level competency balance is purpose-driven;
- programme-level competency progression must be visible across chapter sequence;
- professional_judgment is expected to increase progressively, with strongest operational emphasis in Volume 2.

## 9. Bridge-Objective Policy

### 9.1 Requirement

Advanced technical chapters must include explicit bridge objectives connecting:

- technical execution -> evidential interpretation, and/or
- evidential interpretation -> reliability/limitation evaluation.

### 9.2 Acceptable bridge objective forms

- apply method to interpret evidence quality;
- interpret results to evaluate assumption sensitivity;
- explain why method choice affects reliability of conclusions;
- compare methods to justify evidential adequacy under constraints.

### 9.3 Non-compliant forms

- purely procedural outcome lists with no interpretation endpoint;
- generic interpretation verbs without explicit evidence object;
- chapter sets where all higher-order outcomes are disconnected from method execution.

## 10. Reviewer Responsibilities

Reviewers must:

1. evaluate chapter quality against local standards;
2. evaluate sequence-level progression contribution;
3. identify excessive regularization risks;
4. test whether bridge objectives are explicit and meaningful;
5. document progression concerns even if local compliance is passed;
6. require remediation when curriculum-level progression is weak.

Reviewer authority:

A chapter may be compliant locally yet require revision for curriculum-level progression reasons.

## 11. Author Responsibilities

Authors must:

1. articulate chapter educational purpose explicitly;
2. justify chapter LO architecture against purpose;
3. explain chapter contribution to programme progression;
4. include explicit bridge objectives where chapter role requires them;
5. justify symmetry when chapter profile resembles adjacent chapters strongly;
6. document justified exceptions where temporary complexity reset is pedagogically required.

## 12. Guidance for Curriculum Audits

Curriculum audits should include both local and sequence-level checks.

Required audit lenses:

- chapter purpose clarity;
- progression visibility across chapter transitions;
- bridge-objective presence and quality;
- pattern-level regularization risks;
- alignment with Perform -> Interpret -> Evaluate spine;
- alignment with competency progression intent.

Audit outputs must include:

- findings;
- progression-risk assessment;
- remediation recommendations;
- justified exception records.

## 13. Guidance for Future Chapter Redesign Projects

Chapter redesign proposals must include:

1. chapter role statement in programme progression;
2. expected effect on adjacent chapter transitions;
3. bridge-objective plan;
4. Bloom and competency implications interpreted as indicators;
5. explanation of why profile changes are purpose-led.

Redesign approval should be denied where changes optimize local chapter metrics but degrade sequence-level progression.

## 14. Exception-Handling Guidance

Exceptions are permitted where pedagogically justified.

Legitimate exception cases:

- introduction of a new technique family;
- chapter requiring foundational reset for a distinct conceptual domain;
- temporary reduction in apparent cognitive level to establish prerequisite language.

Exception requirements:

- explicit justification;
- defined scope and duration;
- statement of how progression resumes in subsequent chapters.

## 15. Examples of Compliant and Non-Compliant Patterns

### 15.1 Compliant progression patterns

Pattern A: Purpose-led differentiation

- Chapter N emphasizes Technical Skills and Apply for foundational mastery.
- Chapter N+1 adds Analyze bridge objectives focused on interpretation of method differences.
- Chapter N+2 increases Evaluate through evidence-strength judgments.

Pattern B: Controlled reset with explicit bridge return

- New method-family chapter includes foundational objectives.
- Chapter text and LOs include explicit interpretive bridge outcomes.
- Subsequent chapter clearly escalates interpretation/evaluation.

### 15.2 Non-compliant progression patterns

Pattern X: Symmetry lock

- Adjacent chapters share near-identical LO shapes, Bloom profiles, and competency splits without pedagogical justification.

Pattern Y: Procedural tunnel

- Advanced chapter has strong technical execution outcomes but no explicit bridge to evidential interpretation.

Pattern Z: Metric compliance without development

- Chapter passes local LO-format rules and taxonomy checks, but sequence-level review finds no visible developmental movement.

## 16. Consequences for Future Governance Decisions

CURR-022 establishes precedent that:

1. curriculum-governance decisions may override local optimization when sequence-level progression is compromised;
2. progression visibility is a first-order curriculum-quality criterion;
3. future decisions on chapter architecture, LO audits, and redesign approval must include explicit progression analysis;
4. Bloom and competency frameworks remain mandatory but subordinate to developmental educational intent.
