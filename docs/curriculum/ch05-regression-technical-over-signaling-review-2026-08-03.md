# CH05 Regression Analysis Competency Signaling Review (Issue 283)

Date: 2026-08-03  
Scope: Targeted signaling adjudication for Chapter 5 only  
Constraint: Not a full chapter redesign

## 1) Chapter 5 Competency-Positioning Assessment

Chapter 5 is correctly positioned as the expectation-construction and evidence-evaluation hinge between Chapter 4 and Chapter 6, but its current objective wording still signals "procedural regression" more strongly than "evidential reasoning".

Positioning judgment:

- Intended role: construct and evaluate statistically supported expectations for audit evidence.
- Current visible role: mixed, with excess procedural foregrounding in key transition objectives.
- Educational fit with programme spine (Perform -> Interpret -> Evaluate): conceptually strong, operationally under-signaled.

Conclusion: Chapter 5 is a progression-critical transition chapter, and the main issue is signaling clarity, not content scope.

## 2) Analysis of Current Technical Skills Signaling

Current Chapter 5 active competency profile (from LO registry):

- Technical Skills: 8 objectives (LO-C5-01 to LO-C5-05, LO-C5-10 to LO-C5-12)
- Statistical Reasoning: 6 objectives (LO-C5-06 to LO-C5-09, LO-C5-13 to LO-C5-14)
- Professional Judgment: 4 objectives (LO-C5-15 to LO-C5-18)

Technical signal strengths:

- Maintains method fluency for model fitting, diagnostics, and process structure.
- Preserves reproducible procedural competence.

Technical signal weaknesses:

- Some TS-labeled objectives (especially LO-C5-02 and LO-C5-10) describe interpretation-heavy thinking.
- Procedural verbs (identify, use, fit) mask evidential-demand endpoints.
- Classification currently over-communicates execution centrality in a chapter whose educational demand is expectation credibility and evidence meaning.

## 3) Analysis of Current Statistical Reasoning Signaling

Current SR signal is substantial but unevenly visible.

Strengths:

- LO-C5-06 to LO-C5-09 and LO-C5-13 to LO-C5-14 explicitly reference suitability, assumptions, diagnostics, relationships, and adequacy.
- These objectives already carry evidential-interpretive intent.

Weaknesses:

- SR expectations are partially hidden in TS-coded objectives.
- Transition language from model execution to evidential judgment is often implicit rather than explicit.
- Misconception correction is distributed but not consistently foregrounded as a core SR outcome.

Judgment:

- SR is present in substance.
- SR is under-signaled in architecture.

## 4) Detailed Review: LO-C5-02

Current objective:

- LO-C5-02 (current competency: Technical Skills)
- "identify the purpose of regression analysis in substantive analytical procedures and the role of expectations, residuals, and prediction uncertainty in evaluating audit evidence."

Alternative competency interpretation:

- Primary: Statistical Reasoning
- Secondary: Technical Skills

Evidence for change:

- The endpoint is not procedure execution; it is evidential-role interpretation.
- The objective requires conceptual linkage: expectations + residuals + uncertainty -> evidence evaluation.
- "Identify" is a low-signal verb for a high-meaning chain and suppresses visible cognitive demand.

Evidence for retention:

- Foundational terminology and orientation may justify TS support framing at early chapter entry.
- The objective is still partly definitional in surface form.

Progression implications:

- Keeping TS primary weakens Chapter 5 as TS->SR transition hinge.
- Reframing to SR primary clarifies that regression is an evidential reasoning method, not a software routine.

Recommendation:

- Reclassify LO-C5-02 to Statistical Reasoning (primary).
- Keep technical support implicit via terminology, but strengthen wording to make interpretation endpoint explicit.

## 5) Detailed Review: LO-C5-10

Current objective:

- LO-C5-10 (current competency: Technical Skills)
- "use regression output, prediction uncertainty, and materiality to assess whether recorded amounts are consistent with audit expectations."

Alternative competency interpretation:

- Primary: Statistical Reasoning
- Secondary: Technical Skills

Evidence for change:

- Core action is evidential consistency assessment under uncertainty and materiality.
- This is an interpretive synthesis objective, not merely output handling.
- The objective is exactly the chapter's expectation-versus-recorded evidence question.

Evidence for retention:

- Students must still execute output extraction and mechanics to complete the task.
- "Use" can be interpreted as procedural application if assessment prompts remain narrow.

Progression implications:

- SR primary classification makes TS->SR transition visible at the exact chapter pivot.
- Retaining TS primary risks preserving a "run model then stop" reading.

Recommendation:

- Reclassify LO-C5-10 to Statistical Reasoning (primary).
- Preserve method-execution expectations in task design, not in primary competency identity.

## 6) Detailed Review: LO-C5-11

Current objective:

- LO-C5-11 (current competency: Technical Skills)
- "fit a simple and multiple linear regression model to audit data and interpret the estimated coefficients, prediction intervals, and goodness-of-fit measures."

Alternative competency interpretation:

- Hybrid objective: Technical Skills + Statistical Reasoning
- Candidate primary options:
  - keep Technical Skills primary, or
  - move to Statistical Reasoning primary if interpretation is assessed as the dominant endpoint.

Evidence for change:

- The objective includes explicit interpretation of coefficients, intervals, and fit metrics.
- Interpretation element is cognitively central for evidential meaning.

Evidence for retention:

- Model fitting remains a non-trivial execution demand and should remain visible in Chapter 5.
- As a combined objective, it can credibly remain TS primary if interpretation criteria are made explicit in wording/rubrics.

Progression implications:

- If kept TS primary without wording refinement, SR demand stays hidden.
- If treated as explicit bridge objective, it can become a high-value transition anchor.

Recommendation:

- Keep LO-C5-11 as TS primary in the short term, but explicitly mark it as a bridge objective and refine wording to expose interpretation endpoint.
- Revisit primary competency after assessment evidence review.

## 7) Hidden Statistical Reasoning Objectives

Objectives with SR demand currently hidden or muted by procedural wording:

- LO-C5-02: evidential-role interpretation is hidden behind "identify".
- LO-C5-10: uncertainty-materiality consistency judgment is hidden behind "use".
- LO-C5-11: interpretive meaning is bundled after technical fitting.
- LO-C5-12: diagnostic application can become procedural unless tied to reliability judgments.
- LO-C5-05: chapter workflow listing can hide expectation-quality reasoning if treated as checklist only.

## 8) Bridge-Objective Opportunities (TS -> SR)

High-priority bridge candidates:

- LO-C5-10: explicit execution-to-evidence bridge.
- LO-C5-11: fit-and-interpret bridge (currently implicit).
- LO-C5-12: diagnostics-to-reliability bridge.
- LO-C5-05 (secondary): process steps-to-evaluative sequencing bridge.

Example strengthening patterns (without scope expansion):

- Weak: perform a regression analysis.
- Stronger: perform a regression analysis and explain what model diagnostics and uncertainty imply about the plausibility of recorded balances.

- Weak: apply regression diagnostics.
- Stronger: apply regression diagnostics and justify whether the resulting expectation is sufficiently reliable for evidential comparison.

## 9) Recommended Competency Reclassifications

Recommendation type: targeted signaling corrections, not count balancing.

Recommended now:

- LO-C5-02: Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning
- LO-C5-10: Technical Skills -> Statistical Reasoning

Recommended conditional (after rubric evidence check):

- LO-C5-11: keep Technical Skills primary for now; classify as explicit bridge objective with SR secondary emphasis.

Effect on chapter profile if immediate recommendations are applied:

- Technical Skills: 8 -> 6
- Statistical Reasoning: 6 -> 8
- Professional Judgment: unchanged at 4

Interpretation:

- This shift better matches Chapter 5 educational demand without changing chapter scope.

## 10) Recommended Wording Refinements

Goal: improve signaling while preserving instructional content.

Proposed wording refinements:

- LO-C5-02 (signaling refinement)
  - From: identify the purpose ... and the role of expectations, residuals, and prediction uncertainty in evaluating audit evidence.
  - To: explain how expectations, residuals, and prediction uncertainty determine the evidential meaning of regression results in substantive analytical procedures.

- LO-C5-10 (signaling refinement)
  - From: use regression output, prediction uncertainty, and materiality to assess whether recorded amounts are consistent with audit expectations.
  - To: interpret regression output, prediction uncertainty, and materiality thresholds to evaluate whether recorded amounts are evidentially consistent with audit expectations.

- LO-C5-11 (bridge refinement)
  - From: fit a simple and multiple linear regression model ... and interpret ...
  - To: fit simple and multiple regression models and explain what coefficients, prediction intervals, and fit measures imply for expectation reliability.

## 11) Progression Implications

If recommendations are adopted:

- Perform -> Interpret progression becomes more visible in Chapter 5.
- TS -> SR transition appears in objective language rather than only in instructor explanation.
- SR -> PJ handoff to LO-C5-15/16 becomes cleaner because interpretation work is explicitly staged earlier.

If not adopted:

- Students may continue to experience Chapter 5 as a procedural milestone with interpretive expectations treated as implicit add-ons.

## 12) Relationship with Chapter 4 and Chapter 6

Chapter 4 relationship:

- Chapter 4 asks whether a population appears acceptable (hypothesis-based evidential framing).
- Chapter 5 should extend that logic from test outcomes to modeled expectations and uncertainty-aware comparison.

Chapter 6 relationship:

- Chapter 6 asks how anomalies are interpreted responsibly.
- Chapter 5 should provide the expectation-construction discipline that Chapter 6 then stress-tests through anomaly interpretation.

Design implication:

- Chapter 5 should be the bridge between inferential acceptability logic (Chapter 4) and anomaly interpretation responsibility (Chapter 6).

## 13) Relationship with Volume 2 Chapter 1

Volume 2 Chapter 1 asks what actions auditors should take.

Boundary rule:

- Chapter 5 should establish evidential interpretation and reliability judgments.
- Chapter 5 should not require audit response selection as a primary learning endpoint.

Implication:

- Strengthening SR in Chapter 5 supports, rather than replaces, Volume 2 judgment/action pedagogy.

## 14) Risks and Trade-offs

Risks:

- Over-correction risk: downgrading technical visibility too far could weaken execution discipline.
- Assessment misalignment risk: if assessments still reward mechanics only, wording changes alone will not shift learning behavior.
- Classification drift risk: reclassification without rubric evidence could become symbolic rather than instructional.

Trade-offs:

- Clearer SR signaling improves progression coherence but demands stronger interpretation marking criteria in marking guides.
- Hybrid objectives preserve realism but require explicit bridge governance to avoid ambiguous grading.

Mitigation:

- Pair wording updates with small rubric edits (evidence interpretation criteria, uncertainty interpretation criteria, misconception checks).
- Keep TS baseline explicit in model-fitting and diagnostics tasks while making evidential meaning mandatory.

---

## Overall Decision

Chapter 5 is currently over-signaling Technical Skills relative to its true educational purpose.

This over-signaling is concentrated in a small number of high-leverage objectives (especially LO-C5-02 and LO-C5-10), while LO-C5-11 should be treated as a deliberate bridge objective rather than automatically reclassified.

The recommended changes improve competency signaling and TS->SR transition visibility without changing chapter scope, content volume, or chapter identity.
